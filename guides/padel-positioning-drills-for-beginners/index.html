Padel Positioning Drills for Beginners

Most beginner padel mistakes aren't technical — they're positional. A pair that hits clean shots from the wrong spot on court still loses points. Here are the three positioning habits worth drilling first, with diagrams to teach them clearly.

Why Positioning Comes Before Technique for Beginners

Padel's small, glassed-in court punishes bad positioning more severely than most racket sports — there's simply less room to recover from being in the wrong spot. A pair with average shots but correct positioning will consistently beat a pair with better shots and poor court sense. That makes positioning the highest-leverage thing to drill early, before refining technique.

Habit 1 — Get to the Net and Stay There

Beginners instinctively drift back toward the baseline out of caution, especially after a lob. That surrenders the court's most attacking position. Drill: start both players at the net, feed a lob over them, and have them retreat only far enough to play the ball off the back glass before immediately advancing again as a pair — not straggling back one at a time.

Habit 2 — Move as a Parallel Pair

The two players should move side to side together, maintaining a roughly parallel line across the court, not independently. When one player is pulled wide, the other shifts to cover the middle — leaving both players on the same side opens the entire opposite court. Diagram the imaginary line connecting the pair at several points in a rally so players can see the pattern before they're asked to feel it live.

Habit 3 — Read and Cover the Lob

The lob is padel's primary counter to a strong net position, so beginners need a rehearsed response: identify the lob early from the opponent's racket angle, retreat in a straight line (not sideways), and let the ball bounce off the back glass before attacking it. Diagram the retreat path and the ball's bounce-off-glass trajectory together — most beginners try to hit the lob on the fly instead of using the glass, which is the more reliable shot.

Building These Into a Drill Sequence

On the padel tactics board, draw each habit as its own saved play: net positioning with recovery arrows, parallel movement with the connecting line, and lob coverage with the retreat path and glass bounce. Use phases to show the "before" (wrong positioning) and "after" (corrected positioning) side by side — beginners often understand a mistake faster when they see the wrong version first.

FAQ

Padel Positioning — Questions

What's the single most important positioning habit for beginners?

Getting to the net and staying there as a pair. Beginners tend to drift back out of caution, which surrenders the court's most attacking position.

How do I teach parallel movement to a pair that keeps drifting apart?

Diagram both players' positions and the imaginary line connecting them at several points during a rally. Court markers reinforce it physically, but the diagram gives players the mental model first.

Does the padel board show the glass and net correctly?

Yes — the padel court is drawn to regulation dimensions including service boxes and the net line, so positioning drills map directly onto a real court.
