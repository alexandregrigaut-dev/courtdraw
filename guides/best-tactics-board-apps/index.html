Best Tactics Board Apps for Coaches: How to Actually Choose

"Best" depends entirely on what you're trying to do with it. Here's the framework — four real categories of tool, what each one is actually good at, and where each one falls apart.

Start With the Job, Not the App

Every coach searching "best tactics board app" is actually asking one of a few different questions: "how do I show a play to my team," "how do I stop redrawing the same diagrams every week," or "how do I get a play to a player who wasn't at practice." Those are three different jobs, and the tool that wins depends on which one you're solving for. Picking by feature list or star rating skips the part that actually matters — whether the tool fits how you already coach.

The Four Real Categories

Almost every option a coach considers falls into one of four categories, not a flat list of competing apps. Understanding the category tells you most of what you need to know before you even open a specific tool.

What to Actually Check Before Choosing

Once you know which category fits the job, these are the specifics worth checking inside it — the details that separate a tool you'll still be using in six months from one that gets abandoned after two sessions:

  • Your sport(s), specifically. A tool built primarily for one sport and stretched to cover others often gets the court markings or team sizes wrong for the rest. If you coach multiple sports or a multi-sport club, check that every sport you need has accurate, purpose-built markings — not a generic rectangle with your sport's name on it.
  • Offline reliability. Training grounds and gyms have famously bad signal. A tool that needs a live connection to open at all will fail you exactly when you need it — courtside, five minutes before a session.
  • How sharing actually works. Does getting a diagram to a player take one tap, or does it mean exporting, opening another app, and re-attaching? This is the step coaches interact with most often — a small amount of friction here compounds over a season.
  • Multi-step sequences, if you need them. A single static diagram is easy in almost any tool. If you regularly need to show what happens next — a runner's second movement, a rotation after the first pass — check whether the tool supports phases or animation, not just a single frame.
  • What the free tier actually includes. "Free" can mean a genuinely usable starting point, or a crippled trial designed to force an upgrade before you can evaluate anything real. Check the actual limits — court count, saved-play count, export quality — before assuming free means functional.

FAQ

Choosing a Tactics Board — Questions

What's the single most important feature in a tactics board app?

For most coaches, it's how fast a diagram gets from your head to a player's screen. A tool with beautiful drawing but a clunky export/share step gets used less over a season than a simpler tool that shares in one tap.

Do I need a paid tool, or is a free drawing app good enough?

A general-purpose drawing app is genuinely good enough if you only ever need a single static diagram. Once you want accurate court markings, saved/reusable plays, or multi-step sequences, the time spent recreating court geometry by hand in a generic app adds up fast — that's specifically what a dedicated tactics board saves you.

Is video analysis software a replacement for a tactics board?

No — they solve different problems. Video analysis reviews what already happened in real footage; a tactics board plans what should happen next. Many coaches use both: video to review, a tactics board to brief.
